# Commodore 64: hardware research for ISO64

This covers the stock C64 (VIC-II, SID, CIA, 6510), its expansions (REU, GeoRAM, EasyFlash / Kung Fu Flash, sd2iec, SuperCPU) and PAL/NTSC timing, as they bear on ISO64.

The Ultimate 64, C64 Ultimate and Ultimate II+ specifics are in [ultimate.md](ultimate.md).

**Confidence marks:**
- **H**: official documentation, a datasheet-level source, or measured in VICE by this project.
- **M**: a well-known community source (Codebase64, c64-wiki, demo-scene write-ups), not cross-checked.
- **L**: from memory or a single forum post; check before relying on it.
- 🔧: real hardware needed to confirm.
- **VICE ✓**: measured with the probes in [vice-tests/](vice-tests/).

**Main references:**
- Christian Bauer, *The MOS 6567/6569 video controller (VIC-II) and its application in the Commodore 64*: https://www.zimmers.net/cbmpics/cbm/c64/vic-ii.txt (the "VIC article")
- Codebase64 wiki, now at https://codebase.c64.org/ (this machine's network filter redirects the old codebase64.org domain)
- pagetable.com memory map: https://www.pagetable.com/c64ref/c64mem/
- VICE manual: https://vice-emu.sourceforge.io/vice_toc.html, and `doc/vice.pdf` in the local VICE 3.10 install
- Illegal opcodes: https://www.oxyron.de/html/opcodes02.html and https://www.masswerk.at/6502/6502_instruction_set.html

## 1. Timing (PAL vs NTSC)

| | PAL (6569) | NTSC (6567R8) | Old NTSC (6567R56A) |
|---|---|---|---|
| CPU clock | 985,248 Hz | 1,022,727 Hz | 1,022,727 Hz |
| Cycles per line | 63 | 65 | 64 |
| Lines per frame | 312 | 263 | 262 |
| **Cycles per frame** | **19,656** | **17,095** | **16,768** |
| Frame rate | 50.12 Hz | 59.83 Hz | ~60 Hz |

Source: the VIC article. Confidence H.

**How the VIC-II takes cycles from the CPU:**
- **Badline:** 40 cycles, plus up to 3 cycles of BA lead-in, whenever `(raster & 7) == YSCROLL` within lines $30–$F7 while the display is enabled. A standard 25-row screen has 25 badlines per frame. H
- **Sprites:** each enabled sprite on a line costs 2 cycles, plus BA lead-in overhead. Roughly 19 cycles per line with all 8 sprites active. H
- **Measured in VICE ✓** (1000-byte REU fetch started at raster line 60, about 16 lines):
  - +84 cycles with the screen on, i.e. 2 badlines;
  - +421 more cycles with 8 sprites added at y = 60 (+505 over the screen-off run).
- **ISO64 already uses these numbers.** PLATFORMS.md §4 budgets about 1.1k cycles per frame for the 25 bitmap badlines and about 0.6k for 8 sprites over the viewport.

**NTSC risk for ISO64:**
- The REU smooth-scroll frame measures about 15.6k cycles of CPU and DMA, plus about 1.7k of VIC stealing, which is about 17.3k in total.
- That fits PAL's 19,656 cycles but **not NTSC's 17,095**, so on NTSC the walk would drop frames.
- NTSC also has fewer lines. Line 250 is still in the lower border, but the colour fetch that ends around line 285 on PAL wraps to line ~22 on NTSC. That is still above the viewport (line 59), so it is OK.
- **Estimated (M); VICE can verify** this with `x64sc -ntsc`.

**PAL/NTSC detection:** count raster lines (does `$d012` reach 311 or only 262), or time a frame with CIA timers. Source: https://codebase.c64.org/doku.php?id=base:detect_pal_ntsc (M).

## 2. VIC-II graphics tricks

ISO64 uses multicolour bitmap at 160×200. Per 4×8 cell the pixel pairs are:
- `00` = `$d021`;
- `01` / `10` = the screen RAM nibbles;
- `11` = colour RAM.

Hardware sprites are 24×21 hires or 12×21 multicolour, with 2 shared colours (`$d025`/`$d026`) plus 1 per sprite.

| Trick | What it does | Cost / risk | Relevance to ISO64 | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Raster splits** (raster IRQ at `$d012`, `$d01a`) | Change registers mid-screen: colours, mode, `$d018`, sprite positions | ~40–60 cycles per IRQ entry/exit. A stable raster needs the double-IRQ technique (https://codebase.c64.org/doku.php?id=base:double_irq) | The UI panel can use a different mode or colours (e.g. hires text below row 19). Needed for the sprite multiplexer and for music | H |
| **Sprite multiplexing** | Reuse the 8 sprites further down the screen once a sprite's 21 lines have passed. Sort by Y, re-program X/Y/pointer/colour in raster IRQs | Sort ~1–3k cycles per frame (radix or bucket sort). Figures closer than 21 lines vertically can't share a sprite. https://codebase.c64.org/doku.php?id=base:sprite_multiplexing, `base:flexible_32_sprite_multiplexer` | More figures on screen: today it is hero + 7. In an isometric view figures are spread over the viewport's 144 lines, so 16–24 visible figures are realistic | H (technique) |
| **Sprite priority `$d01b`** | A sprite with its bit set is drawn *behind* foreground pixels. In multicolour mode, foreground is pixel pairs `10` and `11`; `00` and `01` count as background | Free. The art convention is the cost | "Figures behind walls" on stock/REU: set the bit for figures whose cell is occluded, and draw occluding wall/tree parts in `10`/`11`. It is approximate: it also puts the figure behind *any* `10`/`11` floor pixels inside the sprite box | H (hardware) |
| **Mask-sprite trick** | A lower-numbered sprite with `$d01b` set and colour = background hides higher-numbered sprites wherever it is opaque, while foreground bitmap pixels still show through it | Uses one more sprite per occluded figure | Exact occlusion against background-coloured pixels too. Mostly superseded by software masking on the Ultimate | M |
| **Sprite overlay** | 2 sprites per figure: hires outline + multicolour body. 4–5 colours per figure | Doubles sprite use (needs the multiplexer) | Nicer figures | H |
| **FLD** (flexible line distance) | Delay badlines by bumping YSCROLL so the display moves down without moving memory | Cheap per frame. Must hold during the area being pushed | Vertical "bob" of the whole view; scrolling a text panel. Not a general scroller | H (https://codebase.c64.org/doku.php?id=base:fld) |
| **Line crunch** | Abort a badline to skip character rows: scrolls the display *up* without copying | Needs cycle-exact code at the top of the screen | Part of AGSP | H |
| **VSP / DMA delay** | Start a badline mid-line to shift the video matrix fetch horizontally by whole characters: horizontal hardware scroll | **Crashes some C64s** (DRAM refresh/bus conflict) unless "safe VSP" memory precautions are taken. Linus Åkesson's analysis: https://www.linusakesson.net/scene/safevsp/index.php | Horizontal scrolling with no copy. **ISO64 rule already in place:** `$d011` is never written during lines `$30–$f7` (the REU walk flips at line 250 for this reason) | H (crash), M (safe-VSP recipe) 🔧 |
| **AGSP** (any given screen position) = VSP + line crunch + fine scroll | Scroll a full bitmap in 8 directions with no copying, then redraw only the newly exposed strip | Cycle-exact top-of-screen code. VSP risk as above. Side panels on the same lines scroll with the map | A route to **smooth scrolling on a stock C64** (§8, OPPORTUNITIES S-7). Big rewrite | M (https://codebase.c64.org/doku.php?id=base:agsp_any_given_screen_position) |
| **FLI / AFLI / IFLI / NUFLI** | Force a badline on every line and switch `$d018` per line, so screen RAM (2 of the 3 per-cell colours) changes every raster line | The CPU loses ~40 cycles on every line it is used. 8 screen RAMs (8 KB) per buffer. The 3 leftmost columns show the "FLI bug" | Not affordable on the stock engine (the CPU is the bottleneck). Possible as an Ultimate-only visual idea (turbo + badlines off), but it changes the art format. Low priority | H (https://codebase.c64.org/doku.php?id=base:fli, `base:afli`) |
| **Colour RAM double-buffer** | `$d800` cannot be banked, so either copy it during the border (ISO64's `flip`) or keep colour RAM constant by art convention | 1 byte per cell per change | ISO64 copies the shadow (stock) or DMA-fetches colour RAM (REU) after the bank flip. Already optimal for a bank flip. https://codebase.c64.org/doku.php?id=base:color_ram_double_buffer | H |
| **38-column / 24-row modes** | Hide edge columns/rows for fine scrolling | Free | Don't fit ISO64's inset viewport: the REU walk masks edges in software instead (documented choice) | H |
| **Border opening** (top/bottom; side borders need cycle-exact code) | Show sprites in the border | Raster IRQ at line 249/250 | UI sprites (compass, portraits) in the lower border. Conflicts with the flip at line 250, so it would need merging | H |
| **Interlace** (alternate two frames) | More apparent colours | Flicker. Halves the frame rate of each phase | Not suited to a CRT-less HDMI Ultimate either. Skip | M |

## 3. CPU (6510) techniques

**Unrolled and self-modifying code:**
- ISO64's masked inner loop `lda (rbp),y / and (mp),y / ora (cp),y / sta (rbp),y / iny` costs **23 cycles per byte**.
- With self-modified absolute operands (`lda $xxxx,y / and $xxxx,y / ora $xxxx,y / sta $xxxx,y` = 4+4+4+5, plus `iny`) it drops to **19 cycles per byte**, and to **~17** when the mask and data are interleaved so one index register serves both.
- DESIGN.md already lists this as option 3. H (cycle counts from the 6502 tables)

**Speedcode generation:**
- Generate straight-line code per tile cell at load time (e.g. `lda #imm / sta abs` for opaque cells, at 6 cycles per byte). Codebase: `base:speedcode`, `base:variable_speedcode_runlength`.
- Memory is the limit: 127 tiles × up to 32 cells × ~100 bytes of code doesn't fit. It could work for the few hottest tiles (floor diamonds). M

**Zero-page tables and pointers:**
- `(zp),y` costs 5–6 cycles; `abs,y` costs 4–5.
- ISO64's ZP is nearly full: `$02-$5f` is common and `$60-$87` is the C64 layer.

**Illegal opcodes** (stable on NMOS 6502/6510/8502; source: oxyron):
- **LAX** (load A and X)
- **SAX** (store A AND X)
- **DCP** (DEC + CMP): loop counters in memory
- **ISC/ISB** (INC + SBC)
- **SLO, RLA, SRE, RRA**
- **ANC, ALR/ASR, ARR**
- **SBX/AXS** (`X = (A AND X) - imm`): subtracts from X without touching the carry setup, useful for stepping table indices by 8
- **LAS**

Unstable, don't use: **ANE/XAA ($8B)** and **LXA ($AB)** have a chip-dependent "magic constant". **SHA/SHX/SHY/TAS** do odd AND-with-high-byte things on page crossing.

Where illegal opcodes are unsafe:
- **65C02 / 65816** (SuperCPU): they are NOPs or different instructions.
- **MEGA65 45GS02 native mode:** those opcodes are new instructions.
- **FPGA cores** that don't implement them faithfully. The Ultimate's 6510 core is believed to run the stable set, but its exact unstable behaviour is unverified (L 🔧).
- ca65 enables them with `.setcpu "6502X"`.

The win for ISO64 is small: most of the cost is in memory-to-memory masking, which illegal opcodes don't shorten. Use SBX/LAX in table-walking code where it reads naturally.

**KERNAL out:** ISO64 runs with `$01=$35` while drawing (RAM under `$e000`) and with IRQs off. Any music player must therefore have its own IRQ vector at `$fffe`/`$ffff` (§6).

## 4. REU (1700 / 1764 / 1750 / clones / Ultimate REU) as a blitter

**Registers** (`$df00-$df0a`); sources: https://codebase.c64.org/doku.php?id=base:reu_programming and https://www.c64-wiki.com/wiki/REU. H

| Reg | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `$df00` | Status. Bit 7 IRQ pending, bit 6 end of block, bit 5 verify fault, bit 4 "size" (1 on 1764/1750), bits 3–0 version. Reading clears bits 5–7 |
| `$df01` | Command. Bit 7 execute, bit 5 autoload, bit 4 = 1 start now (0 = wait for a write to `$ff00`), bits 1–0: 00 stash (C64→REU), 01 fetch, 10 swap, 11 verify |
| `$df02/03` | C64 address |
| `$df04/05/06` | REU address (24-bit; on a stock 1750 only bank bits 0–2 exist, the rest read 1) |
| `$df07/08` | Length (0 = 64 KB) |
| `$df09` | IRQ mask. Bit 7 enable, 6 end of block, 5 verify error |
| `$df0a` | Address control. Bit 7 fix the C64 address, bit 6 fix the REU address |

**Speed:** 1 byte per cycle while the CPU is halted. Swap costs 2 cycles per byte.

**Measured in VICE ✓** (`reu-timing.s`, 1000 bytes, setup overhead subtracted):

| Op | Cycles | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| stash | 1003 | |
| fetch | 1003 | |
| verify (equal) | 1003 | Status `$50`: EOB + size bit, no fault |
| swap | 2003 | 2 cycles per byte |
| fetch, C64 address fixed (`$df0a=$80`) | 1003 | Many REU bytes into one C64 address |
| fetch, REU address fixed (`$df0a=$40`) | 1003 | **A fill**: one REU byte repeated over a C64 range |
| fetch with the screen on | 1087 | The DMA pauses on badlines |
| fetch with the screen on + 8 sprites | 1508 | The DMA pauses for sprite fetches too |

**Blitter uses:**
- **Fill** (`$df0a=$40`, fetch): ISO64 already clears hidden rows with a fetch that repeats REU byte `$00000`.
- **Register streaming** (`$df0a=$80`): DMA a table into one I/O register at 1 byte per cycle, e.g. `$d020` / `$d021` / `$d016` per cycle or per line for colour splits, or `$d418` for digis. Demos use this. M
- **Swap:** exchange buffers in place (e.g. save a UI region under a popup) at 2 cycles per byte.
- **Verify:** fast compare (e.g. "did this window change?"). It stops at the first difference and sets bit 5.
- **The `$ff00` trigger:** set up the command, then start it from a precise moment, or while the KERNAL/IO is banked out, by writing `$ff00`. ISO64 doesn't need it.

**Gotchas:**
- With autoload off, the address and length registers count on after the transfer. ISO64 uses autoload (`$b0`/`$b1`).
- DMA into `$d000-$dfff` sees I/O if `$01` maps I/O in. The colour RAM fetch relies on that.
- Size detection: write signatures at 64 KB steps and look for wrap-around. ISO64 assumes ≥ 128 KB and doesn't probe.
- **Detection:** ISO64 writes two patterns to `$df02-$df05` and reads them back. A stock C64 reads open bus. ✓ (already in the engine)
- **IRQ latency:** an IRQ during a DMA waits until the transfer ends. With ISO64's ≤ 200-byte rows that's ≤ 200 cycles. Fine for music.

**Sizes:**
- 1700 = 128 KB, 1764 = 256 KB, 1750 = 512 KB. Clones and the 1750 XL go to 2 MB.
- The Ultimate REU goes up to 16 MB.
- VICE takes `-reusize` 128 … 16384. H
- ISO64's world image is 121,360 bytes, so it fits every model.

## 5. Other expansions

**GeoRAM** (BBG GeoRAM, NeoRAM, the C64 Ultimate's GeoRAM "soon"):
- 512 KB – 4 MB, seen as a 256-byte window at `$de00-$deff`.
- `$dffe` selects the page (0–63) within the 16 KB block; `$dfff` selects the block.
- No DMA: the CPU copies at ~8–10 cycles per byte with page switches.
- As a "pre-rendered world" store, a step costs ~40k cycles (4.3 KB copied), about 4× faster than the stock 175k but 2× slower than the REU's ~20k. Smooth scrolling (6 KB per frame ≈ 55k cycles) doesn't fit in a frame.
- VICE: `-georam -georamsize 512…4096`.
- Confidence M for the register layout (VICE manual, c64-wiki); sizes H.

**EasyFlash / EasyFlash 3 / Kung Fu Flash** (EasyFlash cartridge type):
- Up to 1 MB of flash in 64 banks × 16 KB.
- Seen at ROML `$8000-$9fff` and ROMH `$a000-$bfff`, or in Ultimax mode at `$e000`.
- `$de00` selects the bank; `$de02` sets the mode (GAME/EXROM/LED).
- 256 bytes of RAM at `$df00-$dfff`. The **EAPI** routines write flash (savegames).
- **Same use as the REU, but from ROM:** the CPU copies the window from the cartridge at ~8 cycles per byte. A step copies 18×192 bitmap + 18×24 screen + 18×24 colour bytes = 4.3 KB, about 38k cycles against stock's 175k.
- The world image would be made by the **editor**, which already renders exactly what the engine draws (proven by the harness).
- 121 KB per map means about 7 maps per MB uncompressed, or more with a per-map bounding box.
- Conflict: ISO64 loads `TILES` at `$8000-$bfff`, which overlaps the cartridge window, so the cartridge build needs a different layout.
- VICE: `-cartcrt file.crt`. M
- Kung Fu Flash source: https://codeberg.org/KimJorgensen/KungFuFlash (H). It supports EasyFlash CRTs, D64/D71/D81 and PRG. **Its disk emulation hooks the KERNAL vectors, so fast loaders do not work.** ISO64 uses KERNAL `LOAD`, which is fine.
- **One cartridge port:** Kung Fu Flash and a 17xx REU can't both be plugged in. KFF owners are "stock + cartridge".

**sd2iec** (https://www.sd2iec.de/):
- An IEC device using SD cards. Over the standard serial protocol it is about as fast as a 1541 on the C64 side, ~0.4–1 KB/s.
- Built-in support for **JiffyDOS** and several fast-loader protocols (Final Cartridge III, EPYX FastLoad, Dreamload, ULoad M3, GEOS, ELoad) (M).
- Lowercase host filenames show up as normal uppercase PETSCII names. ISO64 already exports lowercase names and loads with wildcards (`TILES*`), so it works on sd2iec and on VICE's directory device alike (H, project-verified).
- **1571/1581 burst (fast serial) is not supported** by sd2iec (L 🔧).

**SuperCPU** (note only):
- A 20 MHz 65816. 65C02/65816 opcodes replace the 6510 illegals, so **don't use illegal opcodes** in code meant to run there.
- Register writes: `$d07a` normal speed, `$d07b` turbo (20 MHz); `$d0bc` is a detection register.
- The REU works through the SuperCPU but only at 1 MHz bus speed.
- VICE emulates it as `xscpu64`.
- The Ultimate 64 copies the `$d07a`/`$d07b` interface (ultimate.md).
- Low priority: few owners. M

**Fast loading in general:**
- ISO64 loads `TILES` (16 KB), `SPRITES` and `MAPxx` with KERNAL `LOAD`, about 20 KB in all, which takes 20–50 s on a stock 1541-speed bus.
- Options, most to least compatible:
  1. Keep KERNAL LOAD. It works everywhere: JiffyDOS ROMs, sd2iec, KFF, the Ultimate's IEC and drive emulation.
  2. Ship an EasyFlash CRT (instant).
  3. On the Ultimate, read files through the Command Interface DOS target (~100+ KB/s; ultimate.md).
  4. A custom IEC fast loader (Krill, Bitfire, Spindle): fastest on a real 1541, but **breaks on KFF** and only partly works on sd2iec. Not recommended for ISO64's target set.

## 6. SID

- **Registers:** `$d400-$d41c`: 3 voices (frequency, pulse width, control, ADSR), filter `$d415-$d417`, volume/filter mode `$d418`, `$d41b` OSC3 / `$d41c` ENV3 readback. H
- **Models:** 6581 and 8580 differ in filter curve, combined waveforms and digi behaviour. Detect them through combined-waveform readback in OSC3: https://codebase.c64.org/doku.php?id=base:detecting_sid_type (M).

**Music player in IRQ:**
- A typical player (GoatTracker, SID-Wizard, DefMON) costs 1.5–4k cycles per frame, about 25–60 raster lines, called once per frame (50 Hz).
- **ISO64 constraint:** IRQs are off while drawing (`sei` during the ~175 ms stock step), so music would stop for 9 frames on every step.
- Fix: an own IRQ handler at `$fffe` that works with `$01=$35`, with the KERNAL keyboard scan replaced by a CIA scan or done only in `main_loop`.
- Place the music IRQ at about line 205–215 (below the viewport, before the REU walk's flip at 250).
- The REU DMA halts the CPU for up to ~200 cycles, which only delays the IRQ slightly.

**Digis:**
- 4-bit samples through the `$d418` volume nibble. On the 8580 they need a DC offset (voice-3 tricks): https://codebase.c64.org/doku.php?id=base:digis_r_eazy
- They cost an NMI or IRQ per sample (4–8 kHz means 120–240 interrupts per frame). Too expensive next to the stock renderer.
- Feasible on the Ultimate turbo tier, or with REU register streaming (`$df0a=$80` into `$d418`: one DMA per frame of N samples isn't time-spaced, so it still needs timer-paced writes).

**Dual SID:**
- Common second-SID addresses are `$d420`, `$d500`, `$de00`, `$df00`. VICE: `-sidextra 1 -sid2address 0xd420` (H, VICE manual).
- On a stock C64 the SID is mirrored every 32 bytes through `$d400-$d7ff`, so a write to `$d420` reaches the one SID.
- **Detection:** run voice 3 (noise) on the candidate address and read *its* `$xx1b` while the `$d400` SID's voice 3 is silent. A real second SID shows changing OSC3 values; a mirror shows the `$d400` values. M
- **Stereo player:** GoatTracker 2 Stereo (6 voices) is the standard tool.
- The Ultimate's SID address configuration is in ultimate.md.

## 7. Storage and loading

See §5. Two more points:
- **Map size:** each `MAPxx` load is 3.5 KB (`$e00`), so a few seconds on a stock bus. Acceptable.
- **The REU world build** (5.5M cycles ≈ 5.6 s PAL) happens on every map load. On the Ultimate it can be avoided by DMA-loading a pre-rendered world (ultimate.md).

## 8. What this means for ISO64 (summary)

- **Stock step cost** is mostly strip drawing (57%). The cheapest wins are inner-loop work: self-modifying absolute addressing, coverage skipping, and skipping the copy for cleared rows.
- **Smooth scrolling without an REU** needs either AGSP (big, risky) or a cartridge world (EasyFlash: ~4× faster steps, but not 50 fps).
- **Figures behind walls:** `$d01b` plus an art convention gives a cheap approximation on every tier.
- **More figures:** a raster-IRQ sprite multiplexer.
- **Music** needs the IRQ restructure first, then a player.
- **NTSC:** the REU walk budget is over by ~1%. Measure in VICE, then optimise or reduce `ssh` on NTSC.

Ranked items with costs are in [OPPORTUNITIES.md](OPPORTUNITIES.md).
